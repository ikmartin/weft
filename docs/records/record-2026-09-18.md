# 2026-09-18 — M1: the store and the crawl

M1 of [plan 0.1](../plan-0.1-weft.md) is built and run on all three corpora. The crawl is ported from loom (`refs/crawl/*`, `refs/identity.py`, `refs/resolve.py`, `scan/bib.py`, the download helpers of `digest/fetch.py`) and refactored: settings instead of a quilt, seeds instead of a bibliography, binding instead of candidates, one rate budget per host, and an offline transport so no test touches the network.

## What is built

`weft init`, `weft crawl plan|fetch|status`, `weft survey`, `weft index rebuild|counts`, `weft bib`. The work/version/result model with URI-shaped keys (`arxiv:1709.09864v3#thm-4.1`), files under `works/` as the truth, a SQLite index behind one `Store` interface, and `rebuild` replaying the files into it. 123 tests, one of them network-marked and skipped by default; ruff and mypy clean.

## Measurements

**relloc — a real bibliography.** 22 entries became **20 works**: 15 with arXiv sources, 5 metadata-only, **3 bound by lookup, 2 unidentified (10%)**. loom, on the same bibliography, reports 3 works unresolved of 22 entries (23%) because it records candidates and refuses to bind; weft binds a strong match, which is the difference.

**ACGS — one work to depth 2.** The seed declares its arXiv id, and zbMATH gave its journal DOI, which became the work's key while its home stayed `arxiv/1709.09864`. The survey took **2 minutes** and found **48 cited works**. The plan at depth 2 found **45 works: 38 downloadable (35 arXiv, 3 open copies), 7 metadata-only, 4 excluded, 36 bound by lookup, 2 unidentified**, from 38 lookups.

**The survey reproduces loom's argument against a default subject filter.** By primary MSC family the cited works are `14N 10 · 14H 8 · 14T 4 · 14- 4 · 14F 3 · 14A 3 · 14D 3 …`; by *any* of their codes `14N 19 · 14H 18 · 14D 16 · 14A 14 · 14T 11 …`. A default taken from the seeds' primary codes would have dropped most of what the seed actually leans on.

## Findings

**A subject filter silently drops what nothing classified.** All 4 of ACGS's excluded references were excluded for having neither an MSC code nor an arXiv category: `arxiv:1301.4748` (Holomorphic curves in exploded manifolds), `arxiv:1703.05433` (Tropical gluing formulae for Gromov-Witten invariants), `arxiv:math/9907124` (a withdrawn paper on log modifications) and `doi:10.7301/z0hq3x99` (Ulirsch's thesis). Two are plainly in the seed's own subject. The filter was not judging them; it had nothing to judge them by. A later rule should keep an unclassified work that something cites more than once, or list it in the plan for a decision rather than dropping it.

**One work reached twice by different routes can be both identified and not.** Ulirsch's thesis appears as the excluded `doi:10.7301/z0hq3x99` and again as an unidentified `\bibitem` whose printed text carries a typo ("Brown Univrsity") and no identifier. Merging records under every identifier does not help when one route produces no identifier at all.

**A version directory must be named against the work's home, not its key.** ACGS is homed under its arXiv id and keyed by its journal DOI, which first produced `works/arxiv/1709.09864/1709.09864/src`. The rule is now what the identifier adds to the home: `main` for the work itself, `v2` for a later version, `arxiv-1901.00001` for an artifact of another scheme.

**An arXiv id without a version number names "the latest".** The seed says `arxiv:1709.09864`, so that is the version recorded, though the bytes are of whatever version arXiv served. Results belong to versions, so M2 needs the real number: one API call per arXiv work during enrichment.

**arXiv throttles bulk e-print downloads, and the refusal is a 406.** A fetch of 15 sources was refused after one download; a single probe ten minutes later was still refused; twenty minutes later the same URL answered 200 to curl, to urllib with no headers, and to weft alike. So the refusal is neither a missing header nor a user-agent: it is rate. `Accept: */*` is worth sending (urllib sends none by default) but it is not the discriminator, and an earlier reading of loom's 2026-09-17 incident as a header problem was wrong — that hour was throttling too.

Measured afterwards: a fetch at **17 seconds between requests downloaded five sources and was then refused**, three in a row, which ended the run; every failing URL answered 200 to curl and to weft a minute later; the next runs downloaded five more, then one more. So the endpoint refuses in bursts and recovers in about a minute, and a corpus is filled by resuming rather than by waiting.

**That number is confounded, and the confounding is the lesson.** loom was fetching e-prints from the same machine while weft was, so arXiv saw roughly twice the rate either tool intended: a service throttles a client, and two processes are one client. Nothing about the count of five is a property of arXiv. What follows is the fix: the host budget now records its last request per host under `$WEFT_STATE_DIR`, else `~/.cache/weft/hosts/`, so two weft processes hold each other back; `WEFT_STATE_DIR=` empty turns sharing off, which is what the tests do. Any other tool that fetches from the same machine — loom's own `digest fetch` while it still has one — is outside that budget, so the rate must be re-measured with nothing else running before any number here is trusted.

Consequences, now in the code: bulk downloads take **15 seconds per host beyond the metadata spacing** (`download.BULK_SPACING`), a **406 is not retried** — asking again sooner only earns another — and the stop after three refusals in a row, ported from loom, ends the fetch and says to resume. For thousands of works this is the argument for arXiv's bulk access, which plan 0.1 §11 defers rather than pretends to solve. Worth knowing for anything that runs weft: **two weft processes do not share the budget**, so a plan and a fetch must not run at once. That is the same mistake, at the process level, that cost loom an hour.

## Still open at the end of M1

- The version number of an arXiv source, as above.
- Unclassified works: kept, offered, or dropped.
- Bulk access for a corpus that wants thousands of sources, rather than one e-print at a time.
- A clean measurement of arXiv's tolerance, with nothing else on the machine fetching.
- Whether a tool outside weft should be able to join the same budget, which would mean publishing where the state lives rather than keeping it private.
